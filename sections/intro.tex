\section{Introduction}
\vspace{-1em}
In-context learning (ICL) is the ability of a model to solve a task from input--output
examples given in the prompt at inference time, without updating its weights. The
``learning'', e.g.\ of $w$ for a linear $f(x)=w^\top x$, happens in the activations
during the forward pass, while the weights only encode the optimisation algorithm. For
example, \cite{vonOswald2023Transformers} show that one layer of linear attention
corresponds exactly to one step of gradient descent. We give a simpler motivation in
Appendix~\ref{app:motivation}.

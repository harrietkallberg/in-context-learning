Black–Scholes cheat sheet

The formula as the textbook writes it, as our code writes it, and every constant the tasks use.

Textbook form

C = S·Φ(d₁) − K·e−rT·Φ(d₂),   d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T),   d₂ = d₁ − σ√T

Source: Black & Scholes 1973; Merton 1973.

Our form: price per unit of spot (bs_call_over_spot)

C/S = Φ(d₁) − em − rT·Φ(d₂),   d₁ = (−m + (r + σ²/2)T) / (σ√T),   m = ln(K/S)

Valid because C(λS, λK) = λ·C(S, K): S and K only enter as K/S.

Facts to remember

  • The stock's expected return doesn't appear in the formula; σ is the only input you can't observe.
  • Price strictly increases in σ, so each price gives one implied volatility.
  • Under Black–Scholes, implied vol is the same for every strike and maturity (flat). Real markets show a skew or smile (Derman; Hull ch. 20).
  • Sensitivity to σ is largest near the money and at long maturities. Far OTM, short-dated options carry almost no information about σ (about 2% of our training options move less than 1% of S across the prior).
  • Sanity values (r = 2%): S = K = 100, T = 1, σ = 0.2 → C = 8.916. S = 100, K = 120, T = 0.5, σ = 0.3 → C = 2.710.
